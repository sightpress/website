The basics

What is a CMS?

A CMS, or content management system, is the tool you use to create, edit and publish a website without writing code.

You write pages, add images and organise menus in an admin area. The CMS stores that content and turns it into the web pages your readers see.

What a CMS does for you

  • Keeps your content, images and translations in one place.
  • Lets several people write, review and publish together.
  • Keeps the look of your site consistent, whoever writes the content.

WordPress is the best-known example. SightPress is a new CMS built so that the admin is as usable with a screen reader or a keyboard as with a mouse.

Our mission

Open to write. Open to read.

To make publishing on the web fully open to blind and low-vision creators, and reading it fully open to everyone, by building a CMS where accessibility is the floor, quality design is the goal, and neither is ever traded for the other.

Accessibility in SightPress is a release requirement, on the same footing as security. It can’t be switched off, and every feature is tested with a keyboard and a screen reader before it ships. SightPress is written in modern, object-oriented PHP and licensed GPL-2.0-or-later, like WordPress.

Who it is for

  • Blind and low-vision creators, who are the primary admin users.
  • Charities, NGOs, councils and public bodies that need to meet WCAG 2.2 AA and the European accessibility standard EN 301 549.
  • Small businesses and individuals who want a site that works for every visitor.
  • Audiences who rely on screen readers, magnifiers, voice control or a keyboard.

What is coming

Major features

Everything a modern organisation needs to publish on the web, in a CMS that works as well with a screen reader as with a mouse.

  • Many sites, one installation

    Run every website your organisation needs from one place, each with its own domains, team, look and settings. Your sites can share one sign-in or keep their users apart.

  • Every language, done properly

    Each site can publish in as many languages as you need, with clean addresses for every language, a translator role limited to the languages someone works in, and clear status for every translation.

  • Three ways to write

    A visual editor, Markdown or HTML, chosen per section and per language. Language and abbreviation marks work in all three, so screen readers pronounce every word correctly.

  • Ready from the first minute

    Tell SightPress who runs the site and it creates the home page, contact page, legal pages and an accessibility statement for you. A new site is never empty.

  • Beautiful, and always readable

    Choose colours, fonts and icons for each site. SightPress refuses colours that are too faint to read and never lets the keyboard focus indicator be removed.

  • Teamwork with safeguards

    Roles for administrators, editors, authors, translators and moderators. Edit a published page as a draft, compare the changes, schedule them, and let an editor approve before anything goes live.

  • Media with captions built in

    Upload images, audio and video, or find free and paid stock images without leaving the admin, with caption, credit and licence already filled in. Files stay private until someone publishes them.

  • Easy to find

    Fast, server-rendered pages with everything search engines and AI answer engines need to understand and cite your content, and help for writing titles and descriptions people click.

  • Secure by default

    HTTPS everywhere, one-time codes instead of email links, passkeys, two-step sign-in for owners, confirmation codes for critical changes and an audit log. No CAPTCHAs, no trackers.

  • Backups that travel

    One encrypted backup format for every site. Restore it on another database or another server, or move a site between installations without losing a thing.

  • Small core, official modules

    A contact form comes built in. Sharing, likes, reviews and moderated comments come as an official module, and anyone can write new modules that keep working when SightPress updates.

  • Runs where you choose

    Ready-made Docker setup, any major database (MariaDB, MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQLite, SQL Server, Oracle or Db2), FrankenPHP, nginx or Apache, and storage on your server or in the cloud.

AI that helps, and never decides

SightPress includes an optional AI assistant for everyday tasks: it drafts content from a short brief, rewrites text in plain language, suggests alt text, titles and descriptions, offers fixes for accessibility checks, starts translations and answers questions about using SightPress.

It is off until you turn it on, and it uses your organisation’s own key with the AI provider you choose, including self-hosted models. It only acts when a person asks, never publishes anything, and everything it writes stays a suggestion until a person accepts it.

Accessible by design

Help to publish accessible content

SightPress helps every author publish content that works for everyone, without getting in the way of work in progress.

  • Images need alt text, or an explicit choice to mark them as decorative.
  • Video needs captions and audio needs a transcript, in every language it is used in.
  • Checks as you save point out skipped headings, vague link text such as “click here”, and tables without headers, with a plain explanation of each.
  • A publish checklist shows what is left to fix, and a site-wide scan catches problems after a design change.
  • Every site gets an accessibility statement, based on the European model, created from its profile.

How we test it

SightPress targets WCAG 2.2 AA for the admin, the sign-in pages, the default theme, the built-in modules and emails, and ATAG 2.0 AA as an authoring tool.

  • Automated accessibility tests on every screen, and a new problem fails the build.
  • Manual runs with NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack, with Dragon and Voice Control, with ZoomText and screen magnifiers, at 200% and 400% zoom and in high-contrast mode.
  • Testing with blind and low-vision users before version 1.0, and after every major change to the admin.
  • Public conformance reports for WCAG 2.2, EN 301 549 and ATAG 2.0 with every release from 1.0.

Prefer a terminal? Every admin task also has a documented command, with plain-text output that reads well in a screen reader.

Looking ahead

Roadmap

Version 1.0 is a complete, accessible CMS. After that, SightPress grows with the people who use it.

Planned for version 1.1

  • Site search that works well with a screen reader, often the main way blind visitors move around a site.
  • Full revision history, with clear before-and-after comparisons and restore from any version.
  • A link checker that finds broken links and links to documents that don’t say their type and size.
  • Checks on uploaded PDFs, and a readability score for each language, with or without AI.
  • Display preferences for visitors (text size, spacing, contrast and motion), without an overlay.
  • An accessible form builder and an accessible audio and video player.

Official modules after 1.0

  • For every site: news with RSS feeds, events, a documents library, newsletters, cookieless analytics with no consent banner, and maps with text directions first.
  • For charities: donations with Gift Aid, volunteers and membership plans.
  • For public bodies: meetings and minutes, consultations, transparency data and emergency alerts.
  • For small businesses: catalogues, bookings and job vacancies.
  • For individuals: podcasts with transcripts, portfolios and CVs.

Payments always happen on the payment provider’s own secure page, so card details never reach your site.

Creator

Hello, I’m Pedro.

Publishing should not be harder because you can’t see the screen.

Pedro Ferreira, founder and lead developer of SightPress.

I am Portuguese. I was born in Lisbon and have lived in London since 2010. I’m an open-source advisor and a specialist in modern web development, with PHP as my primary language.

I’ve spent my career building for the web, working with small agencies and startups, and in the telecom and finance industries. That mix taught me how to move fast with a small team and how to meet the standards of large, regulated organisations.

In 2024 I became blind because of a genetic condition. I learned quickly how to keep working with screen readers and other assistive technologies. But I also found that most major web interfaces, especially the admin screens of content tools, are ugly to work with: cluttered, inconsistent, and built for the mouse and the eye first.

I chose to build a CMS rather than patch an existing one because accessibility works best when it is the foundation, not an add-on. I want to give other blind and low-vision people easy-to-use tools for their creative work, and I want those same tools to help audiences who rely on assistive technology get content that is accessible too. SightPress is open source under the GPL, so anyone can use it, inspect it, improve it and trust it.

I hope creators can write, describe, translate and publish without fighting their tools, and that audiences can read the result with any assistive technology. I’d like to show that a CMS can be accessible and beautiful at once, and that blind and low-vision people can build, and not only use, the tools of the web.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

  • What is SightPress?

    SightPress is an accessible, open-source content management system (CMS) for building and running many websites in many languages from one installation. It is designed with blind and low-vision content creators, and audiences who use assistive technology, in mind.

  • Is SightPress only for blind people?

    No. SightPress is for everyone: charities, small businesses, public bodies and individuals. It is built so that blind and low-vision people can use every part of it, and that makes it clearer and calmer for everybody else too.

  • Can I use SightPress with a screen reader?

    Yes, that is what it is built for. The admin, sign-in pages, default theme, built-in modules and emails target WCAG 2.2 AA, and SightPress as an authoring tool targets ATAG 2.0 AA. Every task works with a keyboard alone and with a screen reader, nothing depends on dragging, colour or a CAPTCHA, and every admin task also has a command-line equivalent.

  • Will SightPress help me publish accessible content?

    Yes. Images need alt text, or an explicit choice to mark them as decorative. Video needs captions and audio needs a transcript. As you save, SightPress points out skipped headings, vague link text and tables without headers, and the publish checklist lists anything left to fix. It never throws away your work in progress.

  • Is SightPress free and open source?

    Yes. SightPress is licensed GPL-2.0-or-later, like WordPress, so anyone can use it, inspect it and improve it. The code is developed in the open on GitHub.

  • What do I need to run SightPress?

    SightPress is written in modern, object-oriented PHP and ships with ready-made Docker files. It works with MariaDB, MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQLite, SQL Server, Oracle and Db2, runs on FrankenPHP, nginx or Apache, and stores files on your own server or in the cloud. It loads no third-party scripts or fonts.

  • Will my site be easy to find in search engines and AI answers?

    SightPress includes SEO and AIO (optimisation for AI-powered search and answers) from the start: fast, server-rendered pages, per-language URLs, hreflang and canonical tags, a multilingual sitemap, structured data, link preview cards and help for writing good titles and descriptions. It uses no tricks: it makes your site clear and eligible, and leaves the content to you.

  • Does SightPress use AI?

    Only if you want it to. The built-in AI assistant is off by default. When you turn it on, it uses your organisation’s own key with the AI provider you choose, including self-hosted models, it only acts when a person asks, and everything it writes stays a suggestion until a person accepts it.

  • When can I try SightPress?

    SightPress has not launched yet. Early test releases will follow the first usable version, together with a public demo site. Email team@sightpress.org to hear when there is something to try, or follow the development on GitHub.
